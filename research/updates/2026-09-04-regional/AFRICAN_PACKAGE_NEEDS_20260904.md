# Eight African-language educational-resource comparisons

Working research, 4 September 2026. These eight profiles extend the worldwide investigation; they are neither the whole of Africa nor a ranked selection. The proposed courses below are research designs, not completed translations or measured treatment effects. Existing independent provision is part of the comparison, not a deduction from a population's educational needs.

## What is worth producing, and for whom?

Three mechanisms recur in the inspected evidence: interrupted learning; a mismatch between instructional language and the language a learner can read; and materials that specify activities without enough explanation, feedback or usable substitutes for unavailable equipment. These mechanisms support complete courses as well as smaller additions. A short workbook is not a substitute for instruction a learner never received. Conversely, an additional complete book can add little for a learner already using a suitable course. These are different access situations, not different valuations of people.

|---|---|---|---|
| Amharic, Ethiopic | Early learning and primary numeracy with explicit progression | Complete low-equipment sequence, explanations and feedback | Reteaching/activity alternatives attached to existing guides |
| West-Central Oromo educational profile, Latin | Play-to-literacy/numeracy progression | Complete illustrated sequence with oral instructions | Daily activity and diagnostic cards for the existing programme |

Each option includes downloadable text, navigable HTML, worked examples, cumulative exercises and explained answers appropriate to its entry level. Preschool uses a caregiver or educator; it is not described as solitary study by a young child. For older learners, independent use is an explicit design option. Phone, print, audio and classroom routes remain separate delivery choices. None of the evidence below establishes universal smartphone or internet access.

## Amharic and Oromo: clearer instruction, not a claim of no textbooks

The World Bank's 2026 foundational-learning review inspected regional materials and implementation. It identifies missing guidance for using assessment to choose the next teaching action, low-equipment adaptation problems in an Oromo preschool plant activity, and a heavy materials burden in an Amharic Grade-3 mathematics lesson. It also identifies positive practices, including Oromia diagnostic tools. Its mission observations distinguish reported textbook availability in Addis Ababa from Oromo Grades 1-4 materials still under development; those are regional, time-specific observations, not a current national inventory. See printed pp. 16, 20 and 55 (PDF pp. 20, 24 and 59) of the [World Bank review](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/cdce381e0517064c80cc1f6ad6ce1735-0510022026/original/20260430-Ethiopia-FL-Final-Report-FINAL.pdf).

The proposed full sequence should make the progression executable: demonstrate a skill, show contrasting examples, ask a diagnostic question, explain likely mistakes, provide another route to the concept, then introduce the next skill. The smaller alternative supplies those missing actions around the actual guide. Both require an exact curriculum map; neither assumes teachers exist in every learner's life. Preschool activities can use drawings, spoken prompts and ordinary objects, with a no-special-equipment alternative. Amharic and Oromo are separate editions. An Oromo regional guide does not prove that every Oromo variety is served by one written profile.

Secondary, vocational, university and research options remain eligible. This bounded review does not establish which advanced subjects are most scarce in either language. It would be an unsupported inference to convert strong early-learning evidence into low advanced-learning need.

## Kinyarwanda: a large, directly documented language-access distinction

Rwanda's 2022 census education report provides an unusually useful language-literacy table. Table C.25 (printed p. 123; PDF p. 147) covers **8,289,582 residents aged 15+** and reports **54.0% literate in Kinyarwanda alone**. Table 7.7 (printed p. 78; PDF p. 102) reports overlapping literacy marginals of 78.3% in Kinyarwanda and 21.2% in English. The table percentages are not exact person counts. The 54.0% cell implies about **4.48 million** in the Kinyarwanda-only category; under nearest-tenth percentage rounding its count is bounded by **4,472,230-4,480,519**. This is reported literacy, not tested scientific-reading proficiency, course demand or a treatment effect. [NISR, Education characteristics of population](https://statistics.gov.rw/sites/default/files/documents/2025-02/Education.pdf)

The education board reports that English became the medium of instruction from lower primary in 2021, and that Kinyarwanda textbooks were translated into English and distributed in print and digitally. It also describes books installed on headteachers' devices. Those distribution accounts do not prove every learner has an individual device. [Rwanda Basic Education Board](https://www.reb.gov.rw/news-detail/translated-books-are-now-available-for-students-and-teachers)

The practical implication is not simply to translate another school book. Compare a complete Kinyarwanda course in scientific explanation, measurement, fractions, graphs and data with an explanatory bilingual layer around existing English-medium provision. For adults, the same foundational concepts can lead into records, percentages and elementary statistics; they should not be forced through child-oriented examples. An OpenStax prealgebra or statistics selection supplies reusable subject structure, while the local curriculum establishes the appropriate school entry point. University and research editions are separate candidates with unknown readership, not presumed irrelevant.

Source precision matters here. The census narrative on printed p. 76 calls the combined literacy categories 78.3%, whereas the complementary none cell and national literacy table give 78.8%; 78.3% is the Kinyarwanda marginal. Its urban narrative also uses a male cell where the both-sex table differs. The analysis uses the labelled tables and preserves those discrepancies. The report's pointer to appendix C.24 is not used as the language table: the actual national language table is C.25. No rounded proportion is published as an exact census count.

## Somali and Tigrinya: complete learning routes after interruption

UNICEF's October 2025 Somalia case study describes daily Somali-literacy and mathematics recovery sessions, with over 12,000 participants in central/southern displaced-person settings and over 6,750 in Somaliland. The first group received four months and the latter eight; the pooled results paragraph describes eight months, so the two exposures are not silently equated. Reported improvements are before-and-after programme observations, not an isolated translation effect. The study documents real instruction and material provision rather than an empty baseline. [UNICEF case study](https://www.unicef.org/media/175126/file/Teaching%20at%20the%20Right%20Level%20in%20Somalia.pdf)

Compare a complete level-based Somali course with a practice supplement for learners who can actually attend the existing programme. A full course needs oral/illustrated entry support before assuming fluent reading, while the supplement may begin from already taught skills. The programme participant count is not the full Somali audience. Maay and other exact language routes are not automatically covered by the Latin-standard Somali profile; displaced learners outside the programme remain eligible.

UNICEF's June 2026 account of Negash describes a school closure lasting over two years, resumed fractions teaching, and reconstruction in Tigray and Amhara. This is evidence of a learning-continuity problem in a defined setting, not a Tigrinya-wide prevalence survey. [UNICEF Ethiopia](https://www.unicef.org/ethiopia/stories/every-child-has-right-learn)

For Tigrinya, compare a complete re-entry-to-secondary sequence with a diagnostic/practice pack where school instruction has resumed. Eritrean, Ethiopian and displaced routes must retain their different curricula and histories. The account's territorial evidence cannot establish every learner's language. Practical skills, university study and access to advanced research remain candidates; no stage is assigned zero need because its denominator is unmeasured.

## Kirundi: practical science can matter beyond examination preparation

The January 2025 Twige Twese announcement identifies preschool/elementary capacity, overage enrolment, disparities and limited ICT use. Its numbers for classrooms, school kits and reintegration are programme targets, not achieved access or measured Kirundi demand. [UNICEF launch account](https://www.unicef.org/burundi/stories/launch-twige-twese-programme-improve-fundamental-education-burundi)

A May 2026 Musenyi account describes school STEM projects involving small-space cultivation and household problems, with examples of learners explaining projects to family members. This is qualitative programme evidence, not a causal estimate of income, nutrition or translation benefit. It supports investigating applied scientific learning rather than restricting the offer to abstract mathematics. [UNICEF Musenyi account](https://www.unicef.org/burundi/stories/musenyi-students-are-turning-their-communitys-challenges-innovative-solutions)

The proposed Kirundi course links observation, plant biology, measurement, simple experiments, tables and record-keeping. A complete sequence supports independent learning; a smaller project pack complements usable classroom instruction. OpenStax biology, prealgebra and introductory statistics provide subject-level starting points, not an agricultural extension manual or proof of local syllabus fit. Technical and health claims in anecdotal accounts are not imported into the proposed edition. French/Kirundi school-language relations and Kinyarwanda overlap require distinct evidence. No mutual-intelligibility multiplier is assumed.

## isiZulu and Sepedi: build on substantial bilingual provision

South Africa reported **11,948 schools** implementing mother-tongue-based bilingual education in 2025, including Grade-4 mathematics and natural-science/technology assessments. This is a school count across languages, not a count of isiZulu or Sepedi learners. [Government statement, 24 November 2025](https://www.gov.za/news/media-statements/minister-siviwe-gwarube-welcomes-writing-mother-tongue-based-bilingual)

The DBE mathematics toolkit supplies language-specific annual teaching plans, terminology, teacher guides and worksheets. isiZulu and Sepedi each have named entries; this is not merely an English catalogue. The exact Sepedi worksheet body has not yet been inspected successfully, so the analysis does not claim missing worked solutions or absent content. [DBE mathematics toolkit](https://www.education.gov.za/Programmes/MTbBE/MTbBEToolkit/MTbBEMathsToolkit.aspx)

For each language, the next comparison is a full independently usable mathematics edition versus a narrower explanation/feedback layer around these materials. The proposed advantage of a full edition is autonomous instruction and a coherent path, not an assertion that South Africa has no local mathematics. The smaller option is meaningful for a pupil already receiving bilingual teaching. Later scientific reading, technical education and university/research access must be investigated separately rather than inferred from Grade-4 provision. The same policy evidence raises isiXhosa, Sesotho, Setswana, Siswati, Tshivenda, Xitsonga and isiNdebele as further comparisons; their absence from this eight-profile packet is an unfinished investigation, not exclusion.

## Calculation and evidence boundaries

For each full course and smaller addition, retain the same eligible population and endpoint. Let their additional useful access be G_F and G_S, and their compatible future costs C_F and C_S. Compare G_F*C_S with G_S*C_F. Existing provision affects which intervention adds access; it does not alter the population's measured need. Costs and effects are unmeasured here. A 100,000-token normalization in the shared constructor is a fixed-size traffic scenario, not a claim that a full course and a supplement cost the same.

All eight exact profiles retain preschool, foundation, secondary, vocational, university and research entries. Nationality, school counts, programme targets and partial regional observations are never converted into language-specific need. The matching JSON preserves source URLs, dates, inspected locators, local PDF hashes, the Rwanda calculation and the full/small alternatives. This is substantive additional evidence for the global portfolio, not completion of that portfolio.
