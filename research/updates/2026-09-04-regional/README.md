# Which educational resources would help? Nineteen further comparisons

**Ongoing, non-exhaustive research. Not the final study or a funding-priority ranking.**

Research update: 4 September 2026. This extends the living notebook. The v0.1 Zenodo PDF remains a dated earlier snapshot of the hypotheses and investigation coverage; it does not contain all findings in this update.

The new work examines eight African-language profiles, six Arabic/Iranic profiles and five signed-language profiles. It asks what learners could actually use, not simply how many people speak a language. Complete courses remain alternatives to shorter supplements. A listed textbook does not prove everyone has usable access; equally, the existence of a learning problem does not prove another translation is the best intervention.

## The clearest new directions

- **Early-learning explanation and feedback:** Amharic and Oromo evidence points to clearer skill progression, practical diagnostic questions and alternatives when classroom equipment is unavailable. Compare a complete low-equipment course with additions to existing materials.
- **Scientific reading in Kinyarwanda:** Rwanda's census reports 54.0% of residents aged 15+ as literate in Kinyarwanda alone. Against its 8,289,582-person frame, that implies roughly 4.48 million people in the reported category, not 4.48 million guaranteed course beneficiaries. English-medium school provision does not settle their scientific or practical-learning access.
- **Learning continuity:** Somali, Tigrinya, MSA, Dari and specific Kurdish routes warrant comparisons of full prerequisite-based courses and smaller re-entry support. Exact location, school history, written variety and existing providers matter. National displacement or exclusion counts are not automatically language-reader counts.
- **Applied science and practical data:** Kirundi school examples motivate observation, measurement, plant science, tables and records. Persian, Tajik and MSA comparisons also retain practical computing/data and university-entry courses. These proposals go beyond translating school mathematics alone.
- **Signed mathematics and computing:** ASL algebra/computing, Libras fractions/research-data literacy, ISL secondary/programming progression, Kenyan Sign Language literacy/practical numeracy, and SASL mathematical-literacy/digital-work routes remain distinct. Hearing-disability totals are not sign-language-user totals, and a dictionary is not a full course.
- **Build on bilingual school materials:** isiZulu and Sepedi already have substantial DBE mathematics provision. Compare autonomous full instruction with smaller explanation and feedback additions; do not infer either no need or an empty local textbook market.

These are evidence-informed proposals. Course-specific readership, additional benefit and compatible production costs remain unmeasured in many cases. Missing measurements do not remove communities from consideration. Preschool, foundation, secondary, vocational, university and research access remain eligible for every profile.

The bounded calculation now contains designs for 44 of the 100 initial forecast profiles, across 112 of 600 possible profile-stage cells. This is research coverage, not educational coverage or a ranking. It includes 214 stage-and-mode routes because full courses, smaller alternatives and educational stages are represented separately; they are not 214 disjoint populations. The wider universe remains open, including communities outside the forecast.

The earlier 46 route results and 36 conditional secondary comparisons are unchanged. The newly added context counts were not substituted for measured need. Fifty-six constructor checks passed; these establish bounded implementation properties, not a completed global omission or effectiveness audit.

The next comparisons must attach defensible costs and possible benefits to actual course sizes, distinguish overlapping learners, and continue investigating populations absent from the current calculation. Earlier local programme completion or spending has no role in global need or eligibility.
