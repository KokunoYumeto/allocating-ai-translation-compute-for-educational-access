# Signed-language educational-package needs: five distinct profiles

Evidence cut-off and access date: 2026-09-04. This is a bounded comparison for the worldwide educational-access paper, not a language ranking or production commitment. The accompanying JSON records sources, population frames, inspection limits and finite candidates. Foundation, secondary, practical/vocational, university and research remain eligible for every profile.

## Direct findings

All five profiles have independently produced educational provision. The meaningful opportunity is not simply to place a signer beside an otherwise unchanged mathematics textbook. It can be a complete, navigable course with explanations, exercises and answers in usable modalities; a smaller missing practice component; or an advanced research resource whose audience is smaller but genuinely underserved. National wealth, disability totals and the existence of a dictionary do not settle this comparison.

The strongest provisional directions are ASL algebra-to-computing access; Libras fraction/ratio learning and research data literacy; ISL secondary continuity and programming transitions; KSL bilingual literacy and practical numeracy; and SASL matric mathematical literacy and digital-work preparation. These are source-informed hypotheses, not measured additional-beneficiary totals. Existing provision substantially weakens claims for generic new glossaries and undifferentiated elementary mathematics translations.

Inspection was of actual provider lesson indices, embedded-resource identities, product contents and selected small primary research/kit documents. No large media were downloaded, and signed performances were not independently assessed for linguistic accuracy. A listed video is evidence of provision, not proof that every learner understands it. Failed playback or document retrieval is recorded as uninspected content, never global absence.

## 1. American Sign Language

**Existing provision is a serious comparator.** RIT/NTID DeafTEC's actual one-variable-equations page supplies three instructional components: equations, equations with fractions, and fractions with grouping symbols. It then explicitly directs learners to Khan Academy practice on one-step, two-step, both-sides and distribution problems. Thus it would be wrong to call this a glossary without practice; the practical limitation is that practice is on another service, not that practice is absent. Its parent index also covers two-variable equations, polynomial multiplication and factoring. [DeafTEC lesson page, undated](https://deaftec.org/teaching-learning/strategies-for-teaching-math/math-video-tutorials/solving-linear-equations-with-one-variable/).

ASLCORE's Computer Science branch contains 139 entries at inspection, including algorithm, array, recursion, testing and time complexity; the individual Repository entry has a video object. It provides a valuable technical lexicon, not a verified complete programming course. A separate paid alternative, Everyday ASL's eleven-film mathematics set, explicitly covers word problems, trigonometry, calculus, complex numbers, probability and statistics, with downloadable films. Its description emphasizes expressing mathematics in ASL; a complete exercise-and-answer curriculum was not established. [ASLCORE CS](https://aslcore.org/computerscience/), [Repository entry](https://aslcore.org/computerscience/entries/?id=repository&navset=1), [Everyday ASL contents](https://everydayasl.com/products/math11).

**Candidate ASL-A: a complete algebra-to-functions self-study course.** Target secondary learners, returning adults and first-year college entrants who use ASL but cannot comfortably learn the subject from English alone. Six finite units would cover equations, ratio, coordinate graphs, systems, polynomials and functions, each with signed explanation, persistent notation, worked examples and locally usable self-checks. Its mechanism is continuity between explanation and independent application. A smaller alternative is an offline practice-and-answer companion to DeafTEC. Choose the full course if prerequisite explanations or signed problem interpretation remain missing for the target route; choose the companion when the existing explanations are usable and the main obstacle is independent practice. Neither conclusion is established by U.S. wealth or resource counts.

**Candidate ASL-B: executable computing and proof-reading modules.** Target vocational IT learners, undergraduate computing students and beginning research readers: six modules on variables/functions, recursion, assertions, tests, algorithmic complexity and proof/counterexample. Pair runnable code and exact written terminology with existing sign references and contextual explanations. An integrated introductory course is a real alternative to merely adding more terms. For research, ASL STEM Wiki already offers 254 interpreted STEM articles, including 26 mathematics articles, and over 300 video hours; its research-use licence and interpreted-English character must be preserved, rather than assuming unrestricted textbook reuse or natural ASL-first authorship. [Original study, 2024-11-08, §§3.1–3.5 and datasheet](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.05783v1).

Foundation remains eligible: an arithmetic/word-problem course for late-access learners could be worthwhile, but existing elementary videos and the paid primer weaken generic duplication. The advanced route is not evidence that all ASL users already have foundational access.

## 2. Libras

**There are developed bilingual mathematics materials.** The UFPel team's 2019 production report describes child-facing addition/subtraction videos, visual stories, Portuguese captions and narration, and a design in which signing is central rather than a tiny inset. Its earlier classification collection was teacher-facing: not every playlist has the same learner population. The 2025 primary content study, using March 2025 observations, identifies 28 fraction videos, thirteen additive-field videos and a substantial glossary; it examines the actual lesson “V67 — Fração como operador multiplicador,” with a signer, mathematical imagery and optional captions. This is stronger evidence than a catalogue title, but not a trial of self-study efficacy. [Production report, pp.1–3](https://cti.ufpel.edu.br/siepe/arquivos/2019/MD_01555.pdf), [content study, Tables 1–2 and Figure 1, pp.2–3](https://anais-siiepe.ufpel.edu.br/2025/MD_01023.pdf).

INES also lists a new *Apostila de Matemática Básica em LIBRAS*, dated 2026-06-03, alongside numerical-sign materials and a 2025 visual manual for scientific-work standards. The mathematics booklet's internal contents were not exposed by the inspected catalogue: it remains explicit counterevidence to absence, but not verified coverage of every proposed unit. [INES products, dated entries](https://mestrado.ines.gov.br/tccs-e-produtos/produtos).

**Candidate LIB-A: a complete fraction-to-ratio visual course**, serving older primary pupils, secondary catch-up learners and adults applying quantities in work. Six units: part–whole and number line, equivalence, operations, fraction as operator, ratio/proportion, and measurement applications. A full edition would integrate explanations, contrasting worked problems, exercises and answers in Libras/video plus written Portuguese and MathML. The narrower alternative is a practice/answer sequence built around existing MathLibras lessons. Existing fraction coverage makes a fresh unconnected explanation series a weak choice; a coherent course still has a defensible mechanism if navigation, progressive practice or offline availability is the binding limitation. Those limitations are candidate-specific hypotheses, not demonstrated universal defects of MathLibras.

**Candidate LIB-B: a bilingual research-data practicum**, for university entrants and novice researchers who know Libras and need to interpret tables, uncertainty and reproducible results. Six units: data dictionaries, descriptive summaries, graphs, sampling, uncertainty and a reproducible notebook. A full practicum includes datasets, code, signed conceptual explanation and answer rationales; a smaller alternative covers only probability/uncertainty terminology and interpretation. INES's scientific-work products demonstrate academic provision, while the inspected resources do not establish a complete independently usable statistics sequence. This makes advanced additionality plausible but less directly evidenced than the school-course comparison. Do not describe advanced material as globally absent.

Practical measurement belongs in LIB-A; foundational language/number work remains separate where needed. Written Portuguese can support bilingual learners and exact symbol lookup but is not itself Libras. The sign-knowledge survey does not tell us how many of its 153,000 subgroup members can use either package, nor how many hearing signers or older adults would benefit.

## 3. Indian Sign Language

**Current institutional provision contradicts stale absence language.** ISLRTC advertises NCERT textbook e-content for Classes I–VI. Its separate Maths Concept Video page contains actual embedded videos under Class VI and Class VII, including identities `v4Msfk4kDQo` and `DxR6Ou6_wHQ`; the first playback request was throttled, so topic-level contents and answers remain uninspected. This is evidence of existing mathematics provision, not evidence of a fully covered national curriculum. [NCERT–ISLRTC resource page](https://islrtc.nic.in/ncert-books-in-isl/), [maths concept index](https://islrtc.nic.in/maths-concept-video/).

NIOS provides a more revealing secondary comparator. Its English 202 index pairs lesson PDFs, sign-language video links, learner guides and worksheets. “Snake Bite,” “How the Squirrel Got Its Stripes” and “Kondiba—A Hero” have signed links. Lesson 6 and numerous later rows show dashes in that column; that is a visible omission in this index, not proof that those lessons lack ISL provision everywhere. A basic course teaching ISL vocabulary, or ISL as examination subject, must not be counted as mathematics/science taught through ISL. [NIOS English 202, rows 1–27, undated](https://rcpune.nios.ac.in/English-202.html).

**Candidate ISL-A: a self-contained secondary quantitative-continuity course.** Target ISL-using adolescents and adult school-returners moving beyond early NCERT material, particularly those needing mathematics without continuous interpretation. Six units: proportional reasoning, algebra, graphs, geometry/measurement, probability and data interpretation; include signed word-problem explanations, worked practice and answers. A full course can address cumulative prerequisites; its smaller alternative fills particular exercise/answer gaps around existing concept videos. Class VII provision means “everything after VI is absent” is not defensible. The exact additionality of Classes VIII–X remains uncertain after this bounded inspection, so the full-course candidate stays provisional rather than receiving an invented advantage.

**Candidate ISL-B: a programming-entry course**, for vocational learners and first-year university students using ISL alongside their actual written-language repertoire. Six units: files/input-output, variables, control flow, functions, debugging/tests and small data tables. Use executable examples and bilingual technical labels; do not assume Hindi, English or any state language is comfortable for all ISL users. The narrower variant is signed navigation and explanation for an existing text course; the full variant supplies its own conceptual sequence and answers. The NIOS mixed-media structure demonstrates a feasible content architecture, not proof that its English-literature effects transport to programming.

At foundation, prioritize a complete child-accessible route through existing NCERT lessons before presuming another arithmetic book is required; late language access can still justify a new beginning course. At research, a finite methods/notebook unit for Deaf graduate learners is eligible, but this review has not established its audience size or advantage over existing university support. No national deafness count is assigned to these stages.

## 4. Kenyan Sign Language

**Literacy provision is substantial and specifically Kenyan.** Studio KSL's Digital StoryTime includes KSL stories, comprehension questions and vocabulary activities, with English/Kiswahili audio and captions in the documented broadcast format. The current provider page offers interactive stories, quizzes and progress tracking; the assessment page embeds episodes 026, 009, 011 and 022. The episode identities were inspected, not their signed performance. The claimed 337 videos/storybooks cover six African sign languages combined, so they are not 337 KSL items. [Studio KSL](https://www.ekitabu.com/studioksl), [episode index](https://www.ekitabu.com/ksl-assessment), [2020 broadcast-format account](https://www.ekitabu.com/news/ekitabus-signed-digital-story-time-included-in-kenya-ministry-of-educations-distance-learning-program).

The 2025 second-season announcement adds a Windows application and web route. General eKitabu documentation supports offline e-book reading, but this does not establish that every embedded KSL video is included offline. This distinction matters for a package whose principal language channel is video, not a few kilobytes of text. [Season 2, 2025-11-15](https://www.ekitabu.com/news/digital-storytime-season-2-is-here), [eKitabu offline-book help](https://support.ekitabu.com/shophelp/ekitabu-shop-homepage).

**Candidate KSL-A: a complete early bilingual literacy pathway**, for primary children acquiring KSL and written English or Kiswahili, including late entrants. Six story-based units progress from matching events and sequence to questions, comparison, inference and independent retelling; include replayable signing, illustrated print and immediate answer explanations. Existing stories with questions mean a new generic story collection is not automatically additional. A smaller alternative is an explicitly sequenced practice/answer booklet with stable episode links. A full offline course has a distinct case if the selected existing story-plus-quiz route cannot operate without connectivity; app installation alone does not prove that condition either way.

**Candidate KSL-B: practical numeracy and digital records**, for secondary catch-up and vocational learners: money/change, quantities, unit conversion, measurement, tables and spreadsheet records. A coherent course joins conceptual explanation to everyday tasks and worked checks; a smaller addition supplies only task-specific signed interpretation around available materials. Digital StoryTime is a literacy comparator, not proof that such a numeracy course already exists. KICD's Grade 7 KSL design establishes school-stage language provision, but a language-subject curriculum is not a student-facing signed subject textbook. [KICD Grade 7 KSL, September 2024](https://kicd.ac.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/KSL-GRADE-7.pdf).

University and research candidates need not be another school textbook: a six-unit reproducible KSL corpus/data-methods reader would cover annotation, sampling, train/test separation, error analysis, uncertainty and reporting. The Kenyan authors' 2024 dataset study supplies a concrete local research baseline, not evidence that all graduates study sign recognition or that an autonomous translator has been solved. This advanced candidate has a specific scholarly use case but weaker evidence of population reach. [Wanzare et al., 2024-10-23](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18295). KSL here always means Kenyan Sign Language, not Korean Sign Language used under the same acronym in some AI datasets.

## 5. South African Sign Language

**Fuller domestic packages already exist.** SLED sells SASL CAPS kits, not just isolated dictionary signs. Its two-page contents brochure specifies illustrated signed stories, games and puzzles; Grade 5 includes 25 DVDs plus learner workbook and teaching guide; other kits address Grade 9 and a bridge to FET. The shop has actual foundation-story sample entries such as “Off to the moon” and “Phuti goes to school,” plus a book/DVD family dictionary. The kits are primarily teacher-mediated language/literature resources; their existence neither proves universal home access nor warrants replacing them. [SLED contents brochure, p.2, March 2019 path](https://www.sled.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Full-SASL-CAPS-kit-pamphet.pdf), [shop, page dated 2021-05-04](https://www.sled.org.za/shop/).

DBE's 2018 spring-school page lists revision booklets and scripted/signed plans for English FAL, Mathematical Literacy and SASL Home Language. A Mathematical Literacy plan resolved to a 59-page PDF, but subsequent content retrieval timed out; exact internal coverage is uninspected. Several literature-slide labels explicitly say ASL, so they must not automatically be treated as SASL. Separately, eDEAF's actual learner hub describes SASL-delivered AET/FLC, IT and retail training, and has information videos on learnerships and work readiness. These are substantive alternatives, although campus support and accredited programmes are not identical to open autonomous materials. [DBE spring-school resources](https://www.education.gov.za/Programmes/InclusiveEducation/SouthAfricanSignLanguage.aspx), [eDEAF learner hub, current inspection](https://edeaf.co.za/learner-hub/).

**Candidate SASL-A: a complete matric mathematical-literacy course**, for secondary learners and adults returning to qualifications: proportional reasoning, finance arithmetic, measurement, maps/scales, data and probability. Each unit pairs SASL explanation with exact notation, realistic tasks and self-check answers. Compare directly with DBE revision resources: a smaller exam-practice edition is preferable only when their explanatory sequence is usable by the target learner. A full standalone edition remains meaningful for learners lacking that sequence; the failed PDF read cannot be used to assert that they lack it.

**Candidate SASL-B: digital-work and data-literacy learning**, serving vocational entrants and university newcomers: operating files, reading task instructions, spreadsheets, checking formulas, interpreting graphs and documenting results. eDEAF already teaches digital fundamentals and related vocational skills; an independent offline course can address access outside its institutional route but cannot claim to replace mentorship, accreditation or employment placement. A smaller troubleshooting/answer pack competes with the complete course, not automatically outranks it.

Foundation remains a legitimate separate route: a home-use sequence connected to SLED literature competes with a newly authored full bilingual reading course. At research level, a data interpretation and academic-writing module for SASL-using graduate readers is eligible, but no exact advanced-mathematics signed edition or its global absence was verified here. The anomalous census percentage must not force this profile below other languages numerically.

## Modalities, offline production and presently supportable AI claims

Text preserves searchable terminology, code and durable answers; sign supplies linguistic explanation for learners for whom written language is not an adequate substitute. Video preserves movement and non-manual information. MathML supplies machine-readable mathematical structure and notation, not ASL, Libras, ISL, KSL or SASL grammar. Use diagrams and separate mathematical panels so the signer does not obscure formulas, and avoid simultaneous text/diagram demands that cannot be paused. W3C explicitly notes that some people want both captions and sign, and that not everyone with hearing loss signs. [W3C sign-language guidance, updated 2024-09-17](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/sign-languages/), [MathML, introduction and design goals](https://www.w3.org/TR/MathML3/chapter1.html).

Current AI support is real but asymmetric. Google's 2026-08-12 SL2T release starts with ASL-to-English input in Gboard/Live Transcribe on Pixel 11. On-device pose extraction sends coordinates to a server; it is not an offline lesson generator. The provider documents residual errors in rare signs, fingerspelling, classifiers and tense, and describes generation/additional languages as future work. Its benchmark is not a learning-effect or access-probability estimate. [Google DeepMind, “How SL2T works” and “Looking ahead”](https://deepmind.google/blog/putting-sign-language-ai-into-users-hands/).

Brazil's free/open-source VLibras automatically translates Portuguese content into Libras avatars, but the government specifically advises against replacement use in courses and classes. SignLLM demonstrates text/prompt-to-pose production; its current project page says the model will not be open-sourced and distinguishes poses from further rendered videos. Its Korean KSL example cannot support Kenyan KSL coverage. These facts rule out treating fluent-looking animation or an English gloss as a verified five-language educational edition, not all provisional machine assistance. [VLibras official description](https://www.gov.br/governodigital/pt-br/acessibilidade-e-usuario/vlibras/vlibras), [SignLLM project, 2025 paper/current page](https://signllm.github.io/).

Immediately feasible work includes structured text/MathML, executable examples, answer checks, video indexing, accessible navigation and packaging of material whose reuse is already lawful. Preserve complete existing signed clips and their provenance; concatenated dictionary signs do not establish a correct sentence. Where redistribution is not granted, a linked edition and original text/practice are a finite alternative, not an indefinite permission hold. Experimental signing can be explicitly labelled and versioned without claiming demonstrated comprehension. Missing human evidence never becomes a release prerequisite, but deterministic checks cannot certify natural-language meaning merely by passing file or formula tests.

An offline edition needs its actual videos, captions, indexes, fonts, mathematical rendering and exercises available locally; a PDF of links or installed app may still depend on the network. Storage and encoding cost must preserve visible hands, face and signing space. Text-only fallbacks help some learners but cannot silently stand in for the signed route when counting access.

For conditional benefit/cost comparison, let a full course F and smaller addition S serve the same specified learner group. Here B is the stage-and-package-matched eligible population and q is its fraction newly gaining the specified usable access, not a test score or net access change. Compare `B_F*q_F/C_F` and `B_S*q_S/C_S`, with matched definitions and disjoint beneficiary accounting. If their eligible populations are the same, F exceeds S only when `q_F/q_S > C_F/C_S` for positive denominators. The present evidence does not identify those probabilities or production costs. For none of the five profiles should unknown sign-user counts, unavailable tokenizer measurements or uncertain video-production performance be replaced by zero benefit. The substantive conclusion is to retain complete courses and targeted additions as competing, language-specific candidates, with actual existing provision—not deafness prevalence or generic AI promises—as the comparator.
