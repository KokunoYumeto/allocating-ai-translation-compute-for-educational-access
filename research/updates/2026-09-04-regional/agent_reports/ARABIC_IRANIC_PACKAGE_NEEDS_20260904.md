# Arabic and Iranic educational-package needs

Research date: 4 September 2026. Six retained profiles; research and package specifications only. No teaching edition, shared calculator change or publication was produced.

## What the evidence supports

There is a strong case for comparing substantial autonomous courses, not just short supplements, for interrupted schooling and excluded learners. There is also substantial independent provision. The useful question is which complete learning route a new package adds: a downloadable book with explanations and answers, a sequential course that starts at the learner's prerequisites, or a particular written variety absent from an otherwise suitable route. A catalogue entry neither proves usable access nor makes another edition unnecessary.

The figures below describe dated territory/stage populations, exclusions or enrolments. None is a count of readers of the proposed edition. Package choices are evidence-informed proposals, not measured translation effects. "Open" for a proposed package means openly licensed, downloadable and reusable. Existing free-to-view resources are not presumed to carry such a licence. Preschool routes require a caregiver or educator; autonomous study applies to learners able to use the material independently.

## 1. Modern Standard Arabic: four separate territory cells

Profile `arb-Arab`; Egyptian, Moroccan, Yemeni and Syrian spoken varieties are not silently substituted for MSA. The World Bank's 2021 Arabic education report identifies the transition from home speech to the written/formal register as an early-learning issue. This supports explicit oral-to-written scaffolding, not a claim that all Arabic speakers can already study an MSA science course. Non-Arabic home languages, signed routes, minority-language schooling and displaced learners remain separate unresolved groups. [World Bank, 29 June 2021](https://www.worldbank.org/en/region/mena/publication/advancing-arabic-language-teaching-and-learning-path-to-reducing-learning-poverty-in-the-middle-east-and-north-africa)

| Territory | Dated stage evidence | What it can support |
|---|---|---|
| Egypt | UNICEF's June 2024 brief projects 2025 populations of 4.4 million aged 4-5, 15.1 million aged 6-11, 6.8 million aged 12-14 and 6.1 million aged 15-17. These use CAPMAS's 2019 low-growth projection, not a 2025 enumeration. | Separate preschool, primary, preparatory and secondary planning envelopes. Do not multiply all primary ages by a Grade 4 assessment rate. |
| Morocco | The World Bank reports preschool enrolment of 78.7% among ages 4-5 in 2024. | The arithmetic complement is 21.3% outside that enrolment measure, not an MSA readership estimate or a demonstrated book-remediable deficit. |
| Yemen | UNICEF reports 3.7 million children aged 5-17 out of school in 2025. | A dated national interruption context for an accelerated route; language, attained skill, location and delivery access remain unknown. |
| Syria | UNICEF's 2025 annual report, published July 2026, reports 2.45 million children out of school and over one million at risk of dropping out. | Distinct interruption and prevention routes. The HTML summary does not specify the out-of-school age interval; do not add these figures to refugee/returnee counts. |

Sources: Egypt, printed pp. 3-4, especially Figure 2 and its projection explanation, [Data Snapshot 10](https://www.unicef.org/egypt/media/11856/file/Education.pdf); Morocco, preschool paragraph, [World Bank country programme](https://www.worldbank.org/ext/en/country/morocco); Yemen, dated 7 May 2025, [safe learning spaces](https://www.unicef.org/yemen/stories/providing-safe-learning-space-children-yemen); Syria, Education section, [Annual Report 2025](https://www.unicef.org/syria/reports/annual-report-2025).

**Across stages.** Preschool needs an oral-language, play and early-science route, not a miniature secondary textbook. Foundational learners need decoding, connected-text comprehension and numeracy before dense scientific prose. Secondary learners with disrupted schooling need complete prerequisite chains in science, not merely examination summaries. Vocational learners need technical reading, measurement, records and digital tools. University entrants can use scientific reading and quantitative/computing preparation; researchers can use reproducible data-analysis and methods materials. The last two are retained candidates: this check did not establish territory-specific MSA subject demand or university language-of-instruction denominators. Moroccan language combinations and displaced Syrians' previous schooling particularly require distinct route metadata, not nationality-based assignment.

**Independent provision changes the comparison.** Madrasa describes free kindergarten-to-Grade-12 science, biology, chemistry, physics and mathematics videos, Arabic lessons, and offline delivery initiatives. Its provider's current overview is sufficient to refute "no Arabic school science provision"; its older video count is not used as a current inventory or audience. Edraak offers free Arabic MOOCs across business and STEM; its exact DAE101 Excel data-analysis course is archived but described as reviewable after registration. The QRF impact-evaluation course explicitly includes Arabic subtitles: it should not be relabelled wholly Arabic spoken instruction. [Madrasa provider](https://www.almaktouminitiatives.org/en/what-we-do/madrasa-e-learning-platform), [QRF Edraak](https://qrf.org/en/who-we-are/affiliated-organizations/edraak), [DAE101](https://courses.edraak.org/courses/course-v1%3AEdraak%2BDAE101%2BT1-2021/course/), [impact-evaluation course](https://www.qrf.org/en/media-center/our-latest-news/now-live-edraak-impact-evaluation-course-tailored-arab-world)

National provision also matters: Egypt has an existing structured Arabic remediation programme; Morocco's Pioneer Schools use structured teaching and targeted remediation; Yemen's accelerated programme teaches condensed language, mathematics, science and social studies. These are actual institutional alternatives, not proof that a learner without a school can obtain an autonomous printed course. [UNICEF Egypt, 31 March 2026](https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/stories/lasting-gains-education-egypt), [World Bank Morocco, 21 May 2026](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2026/05/21/morocco-s-pioneer-schools-advancing-improved-student-learning), [UNICEF Yemen, 26 February 2025](https://www.unicef.org/yemen/stories/reclaiming-dreams)

**Three packages worth comparing.**

1. **Full accelerated science-and-reading course:** a bounded upper-primary/lower-secondary sequence for Yemen and Syria, with entry diagnostics, concise science explanations, worked reasoning, practice, full self-check answers and printable/offline audio components. Keep separate national curriculum maps and no claim of examination equivalence. Compare it with an index plus printable practice attached to existing Madrasa/accelerated lessons. The full edition is justified when the existing sequence is inaccessible or assumes missing prerequisites; the smaller addition is preferable when that sequence is already usable.
2. **Complete caregiver early-learning course:** a sequential MSA story, observation and number-play programme, with explicitly separate oral-support versions for Egypt and Morocco. Compare against existing preschool/parent resources and Madrasa stories. Its target is usable home/centre instruction, not replacing preschool staffing or buildings.
3. **Full practical data course:** spreadsheet literacy, percentages, charts, basic statistics, records and reproducible small projects for older students and vocational/adult learners, with an optional university/research extension. Compare against Edraak before duplicating a generic introductory course. A smaller file-based exercise/feedback pack may suffice for connected Edraak users; an independently usable open edition remains a meaningful alternative for disconnected learners. Employment and research productivity gains are unmeasured.

## 2. Iranian Persian

Profile `pes-Arab`, Iranian written Persian; keep curriculum vocabulary and audio register distinct from Dari. Iran is not a Persian-reader denominator, and Afghan nationality is not a Dari-reader denominator. Minority home languages, Persian-as-additional-language learners, refugees, and people using signed or accessible formats remain explicit route groups.

UNICEF identifies economic, geographical, transport and gender barriers, classroom crowding in refugee-hosting areas, learning-quality concerns, and insufficient ICT equipment/upskilling. Its page is undated and is qualitative programme evidence, not a national prevalence survey. UNHCR reports approximately **400,000 Afghan and Iraqi children enrolled in primary and secondary schools in 2025-26**, with economic, capacity and documentation barriers. This is an enrolled displaced-population context shared with the Dari card, not a count of Persian-speaking children needing a course. In a separate dated disruption report, UNICEF stated on **5 March 2026** that at least **20 schools had reportedly been damaged**. That is an early incident snapshot, not the September damage inventory. [UNICEF education context](https://www.unicef.org/iran/en/education-refugees), [UNHCR, Education section](https://www.unhcr.org/ir/livelihoods), [UNICEF statement](https://www.unicef.org/press-releases/brutality-war-measured-childrens-lives-hostilities-escalate-iran)

**Existing provision is substantive.** Roshd's official Grade 3 experimental-science book description, dated **10 Mordad 1397**, lists fourteen lessons including food, materials, measurement, water, light and forces, with home activities and science-in-life work. This is not evidence that Iranian children lack elementary science books. Its current edition and answer coverage were not audited. The Persian provider page for **Algorithmic Creativity and Python Programming** identifies a full free video-and-exercise course for Grades 7-12, with smartphone participation. The indexed provider text was available; a direct fetch failed with status 526, which is a transport limitation, not proof of closure. [Roshd exact book record](https://www.roshd.ir/olum/Content/%DA%A9%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%88%D9%85-%D8%AA%D8%AC%D8%B1%D8%A8%DB%8C-%D8%B3%D9%88%D9%85-%D8%AF%D8%A8%D8%B3%D8%AA%D8%A7%D9%86), [course provider](https://www.learn-python.ir/)

**Stage disposition and packages.** Preschool retains caregiver oral-language/science play, without a quantified unmet denominator. Foundational and secondary interruption support has a concrete rationale, but no national count of Persian learners lacking the relevant prerequisites was established. Vocational digital/technical preparation matches UNICEF's stated concern; university and research courses remain unquantified candidates, not low-priority exclusions.

1. **Full interruption-ready science course**, at one selected primary/lower-secondary band, combining scientific reading, observation, measurement and worked explanations in printable modules. Compare with an annotated Roshd route plus self-check tasks. The full course should add autonomous instruction and continuity, not recopy a complete official textbook.
2. **Full introductory computing-and-data course**, from file management and algorithms to Python, tables and basic statistical interpretation, with offline code and deterministic exercise tests. Compare directly with the existing free Python course: add a complete statistics/data sequence where it is genuinely distinct, or a smaller offline/prerequisite layer where programming provision already fits. No jobs-per-course estimate is available.
3. **Technical-study and research bridge**, comprising units, graphs, experimental records, scientific reading and reproducible reports. A full first-year practical course and a smaller terminology/problem pack are both retained. They serve different starting levels; a phrase list cannot substitute for missing scientific instruction. Access to laboratories, accreditation and institutional study cannot be supplied by an edition alone.

## 3. Dari

Profile `prs-Arab`; retain Afghan terminology, local curriculum edition and audio variety. A provider's generic "Farsi" interface does not establish Iranian-Persian/Dari interchangeability. Keep Pashto and other Afghan-language routes, refugees abroad and returning students separate; the Persian card's Iran enrolment cell is shared context, not additional Dari reach.

The joint UNICEF/UNESCO release of **8 October 2025** reports over **2.13 million primary-school-aged children out of school as of 2024**, and a **40% decline in male higher-education enrolment between 2019 and 2024**. UNESCO's **11 August 2026** statement estimates **2.4 million girls excluded from secondary education in 2026** and records women's exclusion from university. These are national, differently dated constructs; they cannot be summed into current Dari need. They do establish that preserving secondary, vocational and university learning is not merely optional enrichment. [Joint release](https://www.unicef.org/afghanistan/press-releases/afghanistans-education-system-facing-deepening-crisis-both-girls-and-boys-warn), [UNESCO 2026 statement](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/five-years-unesco-calls-full-restoration-right-education-afghan-girls-and-women)

**Independent alternatives are unusually important here.** Begum advertises the entire Grades 7-12 official curriculum in Dari and Pashto, free videos, an offline-capable mobile app, quizzes and examinations; it also uses radio and satellite television. Darakht-e Danesh offers full self-paced courses, including university-semester courses, practice and assessment, with offline Moodle saving subject to conditions; almost all courses are described as available in Dari, Pashto and English. Its catalogue includes first-year calculus, digital literacy and digital-economy topics. Neither provider's certificate is assumed equivalent to a state qualification, and the language/version of each actual course remains a course-level property. [Begum projects](https://begum.ngo/our-projects), [Darakht-e Danesh courses and FAQ](https://darakhtdanesh.org/en/courses/)

**Three substantial choices.**

1. **Full portable secondary science edition**, beginning with one bounded science sequence rather than claiming all Grades 7-12. Include prerequisite repair, definitions, diagrams, worked reasoning, cumulative practice and complete answers. Compare against Begum's already extensive course. A printed/offline edition can add independent use without continuous video or account dependence; a workbook is sufficient for learners who already have usable Begum instruction. Neither choice resolves formal exclusion.
2. **Full first-year computing and applied-statistics course** for excluded women, interrupted students and returning learners who meet its prerequisites. Include scientific reading, spreadsheet/code alternatives and reproducible projects. Darakht-e Danesh weakens a generic "no university courses" claim; it does not establish complete coverage of this exact sequence. Keep a smaller preparatory course as a separate alternative, not as the automatic ceiling on ambition.
3. **Literacy-to-practical-study course**, combining reading, measurement, records and low-risk business tasks, with an optional caregiver story/science strand for preschool and foundation. UNESCO's current programme explicitly combines literacy with livelihood skills. Retain university-to-research progression through methods and technical reading, but no Dari research-reader count or causal benefit is known. [UNESCO literacy/skills programme, 14 August 2026](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/five-years-keeping-learning-alive-girls-and-women-afghanistan?hub=66974)

## 4. Tajik

Profile `tgk-Cyrl`, Tajik Cyrillic. This is not an automatic transliteration of Iranian Persian or Dari. Other instructional languages, minority home languages, learners abroad and additional-language Tajik readers remain unresolved subgroups; the national preschool statistic is not assigned to them individually.

UNICEF's **December 2025 Situation Analysis**, in its early-childhood improvement discussion, reports **30% preschool enrolment among ages 3-6**, citing the Ministry's **2025 EMIS** (footnote 103). Its January 2021 factsheet had described 30% as a future target: do not confuse that older target with the later reported level. The remaining **70%** is the complement of enrolment, not measured demand for a Tajik-language product. The 2025 PDF's indexed section and footnote were inspectable, but a full PDF fetch failed; this card therefore does not reuse other table-level estimates or nationalize the report's older assessment samples. [2025 analysis](https://www.unicef.org/tajikistan/media/9231/file/Tajikistan_SitAn_2025_ENG.pdf.pdf), [2021 factsheet](https://www.unicef.org/tajikistan/reports/early-childhood-education-tajikistan-0)

The Ministry's **14 August 2025** account says Grades 1-11 textbooks have been made available as PDFs since 2019 and names its electronic library, Maktab Mobile and Kitobdust. This is strong evidence of existing school material, not proof every household has it or every title offers autonomous explanations and answers. No claim of blanket textbook scarcity survives this check. [Ministry textbook account, final domestic-provision paragraphs](https://www.maorif.tj/news/other/istifodai-samarabaxsi-kitoboi-darsii-elektron)

**Stage disposition.** Preschool has the clearest quantified access context. Foundation and secondary retain reading-to-science and prerequisite support; no exact unmet Tajik-language count was established. Vocational learners retain practical digital/data skills, university entrants retain first-year science preparation, and researchers retain reproducible methods. These latter choices are proposals, not demonstrated national subject shortages.

1. **Complete caregiver preschool course**, with oral stories, vocabulary, observation, sorting and number play, printable pictures and optional audio. Compare against existing ministry early-learning provision, not an empty baseline. A full sequence may add usable structure where caregivers lack a preschool route; a shorter activity pack is appropriate when a centre already supplies one.
2. **Full Cyrillic-Tajik introductory science and data course**, linking reading tables and units to experiments, spreadsheets and basic programming. Keep secondary and vocational entry points explicit. Compare with ministry textbooks and Maktab Mobile; the additional value must be worked instruction, offline exercises and feedback rather than another syllabus list. A companion can serve already supported school pupils; independent learners may need the whole course.
3. **University-transition and research-methods edition**, with scientific definitions, quantitative reasoning and reproducible small investigations, plus optional Russian/English terminology cross-references where relevant to the selected route. Cross-references do not presume bilingual proficiency. A complete first-year course remains eligible alongside a smaller prerequisite pack. The bounded check did not verify a matching independent university-course catalogue, so neither absence nor sufficiency is claimed.

## 5. Sorani

Profile `ckb-Arab`, with Kurdistan Region of Iraq (KRI) and Iranian/community routes kept separate. Generic "Kurdish" provision is insufficient for an exact Sorani match. School language, home variety and written competence are different variables.

KRG reports **1,827,572 students in 2025**, across its regional system. This is a shared all-medium territory envelope, not Sorani enrolment. UNHCR reports **48,000 Syrian refugee children enrolled in KRI public schools in 2024-25**, representing **74%** of its school-aged refugee population, with **77% basic-level** and **35% upper-secondary-level** enrolment. The stage percentages are read as stage-specific rates, not shares of the 48,000. If 35% is the enrolled share of the corresponding school-age cohort, its non-enrolment complement is **65%**; the page supplies neither the raw stage denominator nor a net/gross rate-definition footnote. Do not add the refugee cell to regional enrolment or assign it wholly to either Kurdish profile. [KRG, 28 January 2026](https://gov.krd/dmi-en/activities/news-and-press-releases/2026/january/krg-marks-a-major-turning-point-in-the-education-sector-in-2025/), [UNHCR education section](https://www.unhcr.org/iq/sustainable-solutions)

The actual independent route **eWane**, identified by UNHCR, offers Grades 1-12 lessons separately in **Sorani, Badini and Arabic**. This is institution-level evidence of substantial provision, not a lesson-by-lesson audit of every subject, offline mode or reuse licence. Catch-up, preschool and adolescent vocational routes are also identified. [UNHCR REIP FAQ, e-learning item](https://help.unhcr.org/iraq/en/help/education/refugee-education-integration-policy-2/), [eWane](https://www.ewane.krd/)

**Stage disposition and packages.** Preschool retains caregiver oral/written bridging; foundational and secondary routes distinguish uninterrupted pupils from students changing teaching language. Vocational data/record-keeping and university-entry science are viable candidates, while research-methods provision and readership remain unquantified. Iranian Sorani communities must not inherit KRI counts or curricula.

1. **Full science continuity course for a defined transition band**, with Arabic-to-Sorani instructional support where that is the actual school route. Cover definitions, explanatory prose, diagrams, worked tasks and answers, not just a bilingual glossary. Compare against eWane plus existing language classes. Its strongest rationale is continuity of subject instruction when prior schooling and current teaching language differ; not all refugees require Sorani.
2. **Full upper-secondary/university-entry science-and-computing course**, with clear prerequisites and autonomous offline assessment. Compare with existing Sorani school lessons. A smaller diagnostic/problem pack can serve students already well supported by them; a complete course serves a different access situation. The regional student total does not quantify either audience.
3. **Practical data and research-workflow course**, with measurement, tables, small-business records and reproducible analysis as separate entry levels. Keep initial tasks low-risk and avoid pretending a book confers technical certification. A full vocational sequence and a focused methods companion are alternatives whose benefits remain symbolic.

## 6. Kurmanji

Retain at least two written-route variants: **`kmr-Latn`** for a Latin-script edition and **`kmr-Arab` with explicit Badini provenance** for the KRI comparator. These are operational edition labels, not a census division or a claim that every Badini speaker uses the same writing convention. Sorani and generic Kurdish material cannot be silently pooled. Türkiye, Syria, Iraq and displaced/diaspora learners remain separate; no credible combined written-reader count was established here.

There is unusually direct evidence of a particular need. UNHCR's **24 September 2025** Domiz account describes children who speak Kurdish at home but find the local school variety different. For the 2025-26 expansion through Grade 7, instruction changes from Arabic to Kurdish; familiar science, history and geography become harder when the instructional language is not understood. This is qualitative evidence from a defined setting, not a measured effect size for a Kurmanji translation. The reported **5,033** language-class participants combine students, teachers and parents, so they are not a child-need denominator. [UNHCR language-transition account](https://www.unhcr.org/iq/news/stories/kurdish-language-classes-help-refugee-students-succeed-school)

**Existing alternatives differ by route.** eWane explicitly includes Badini Grades 1-12. Türkiye's Ministry of Education's **2016-17** list explicitly includes elective Kurmanci language materials for Grades 5, 6 and 7. These are dated language-course records, not a current full Kurmanji-medium science curriculum. A 2026 Ministry academy page also lists Kurdish-language teacher-training topics, without establishing all-subject Kurmanji provision. [2016-17 official list](https://yenifakili.meb.gov.tr/www/2016-2017-egitim-ogretim-yilinda-okutulacak-ilk-ve-orta-ogretim-ders-kitaplari/icerik/133), [2026 teacher-training page](https://akademi.meb.gov.tr/www/yasayan-diller-ve-lehceler/icerik/169)

**Three retained packages.**

1. **Full local-variety science transition course**, targeted first to the documented Arabic-to-Kurdish school switch, with exact destination variety/script recorded. Compare against Badini eWane and existing language classes; add a complete teaching sequence if a glossary cannot bridge both subject and reading gaps. The KRI refugee context is shared with Sorani, not counted twice.
2. **Full Latin-Kurmanji general-science and digital-skills course**, with foundational reading support and a defined secondary/adult entry level. Compare against actual source-community curriculum materials and learners' usable Turkish/Arabic routes; the present bounded check does not establish the absence of other Kurmanji science books. Its rationale is an independently usable edition in the chosen written route, not a numerical scarcity rank. A smaller scientific-reading companion remains a meaningful alternative for bilingual learners already using a complete course.
3. **A preschool-to-foundation caregiver sequence, followed by a distinct university/research bridge.** These are two entry components, not one book for every age: picture/audio observation and early literacy for children; definitions, graphs, technical reading and reproducible data exercises for advanced learners. The latter can be a complete introductory methods course or a compact companion. No language-family intelligibility rate, university need count or proof-comprehension transfer is assumed.

## Finite comparison and next calculation

Preserve all six profiles and every stage even where counts are unknown. For a specific territory-stage route, let `N` denote its dated source population and `q` the unknown joint fraction with the relevant written-language route, prerequisite need and usable delivery. Then `N*q` is a conditional planning quantity, not an estimate obtained by assuming independence between those properties. For enrolled KRI pupils, the union served by Sorani and Badini editions cannot exceed the single regional envelope; overlapping users are counted once. Out-of-school and diaspora routes require their own denominators.

Compare a full course `F` and a smaller addition `S` using the same incremental educational-benefit construct and new production/delivery costs: `B_F/B_S > C_F/C_S` when both denominators are positive. Otherwise compare `B_F*C_S` with `B_S*C_F`. No benefit, cost or retained-comprehension rate is invented here, and token ratios are not compute or currency. The next finite step is one exact subject/entry-level comparison per profile: map the proposed complete sequence against the named independent provision, record missing explanations/exercises/answers/offline paths and explicit reuse rights, then calculate conditional break-even thresholds. Missing human evidence is an uncertainty, not a hold.

Validation boundary: the matching JSON records sources, source locators, evidence levels, shared cells, six-stage dispositions and package alternatives. No corpus, large PDF collection or filesystem history was scanned. Some provider/PDF direct fetches failed; indexed primary evidence is labelled rather than promoted to a current download test. None of these failures is used to infer scarcity, zero benefit or a language rank.
