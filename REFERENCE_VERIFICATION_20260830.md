# Reference verification for the 2026-08-30 revision

Scope: bibliographic and claim-support verification only. The paper itself was not edited. Metadata below was checked against publisher pages, official proceedings/repositories, or the issuing statistical/education agency. Titles retain the source's wording; APA entries use sentence case.

## Corrections that should be made in `PAPER.md`

1. Restore the names **Wikström Hultdin** and **Österholm** in both text and references. The current spellings `Wikstrom Hultdin` and `Osterholm` are not exact.
2. Change **Li et al. (2025)** to **Li et al. (2024)**. The authoritative proceedings title is **“Autoformalize Mathematical Statements by Symbolic Equivalence and Semantic Consistency”**, not “Autoformalizing …”. It is a NeurIPS 2024 paper, volume 37, pages 53598–53625, DOI 10.52202/079017-1697. The Microsoft Research page's “June 2025” date is not the conference-publication year.
3. A more exact summary of Wikström Hultdin et al. is: **“The authors coded 4,242 symbol structures in 180 randomly selected Swedish textbook pages; 82% were connected to natural language.”** The present wording can be read as if all 4,242 structures were themselves structural connections.
4. Treat **ASER 2024** as the survey/report name but cite its publication as **ASER Centre (2025)**: the report title page says “Provisional” and “January 28, 2025.” Likewise, **ASER Pakistan 2023** is a survey/report name published on 8 March 2024.
5. The Pakistan Bureau of Statistics Table 11 PDFs expose the census reference year (2023) but no separate publication date. Using 2023 in the bibliography is a defensible dataset-year convention, not a verified publication date; the current official PDF responses report `Last-Modified: 22 January 2025`.

## Scholarly literature

### Shepherd and van de Sande (2014)

**APA-ready reference**

Shepherd, M. D., & van de Sande, C. (2014). Reading mathematics for understanding—From novice to expert. *The Journal of Mathematical Behavior, 35*, 74–86. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmathb.2014.06.003

**Primary records**

- Publisher record: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0732312314000339
- Author-affiliated institutional record: https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/reading-mathematics-for-understanding-from-novice-to-expert/

**Claim supported:** Mathematically advanced readers in this qualitative study used reading strategies different from first-year undergraduates; expert readers relied on disciplinary knowledge, examples, notation repair, and sustained self-checking.

**Boundary:** The new sample comprised three faculty members and three graduate students and was compared with first-year readers from an earlier study. It supports an expertise-sensitive reading prior, not a measured cross-language or interlanguage comprehension rate.

### Wikström Hultdin et al. (2023)

**APA-ready reference**

Wikström Hultdin, U., Bergqvist, E., Bergqvist, T., Vingsle, L., & Österholm, M. (2023). Applying a new framework of connections between mathematical symbols and natural language. *The Journal of Mathematical Behavior, 72*, Article 101097. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmathb.2023.101097

**Primary records**

- Publisher record: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0732312323000676
- Author-university full text: https://umu.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2%3A1852181/FULLTEXT03.pdf
- Umeå project bibliography: https://www.umu.se/en/research/projects/the-role-of-natural-language-when-learning-the-symbolic-language-of-mathematics/

**Claim supported:** The study analyzed 180 randomly selected Swedish mathematics-textbook pages for school years 2, 5, and 8, coded 4,242 symbol structures, and found 82% connected to natural language. It directly supports the claim that readers frequently have to coordinate words and symbols and that the structural pattern changes with school level.

**Boundary:** This is a Swedish textbook-structure study, not a test of multilingual comprehension or an interlanguage.

### Österholm (2006)

**APA-ready reference**

Österholm, M. (2006). Characterizing reading comprehension of mathematical texts. *Educational Studies in Mathematics, 63*(3), 325–346. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10649-005-9016-y

**Primary record**

- Publisher record: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10649-005-9016-y

**Claim supported:** In a study of 95 upper-secondary and university students reading group-theory material, comprehension differed between a symbol-bearing mathematical text and a natural-language-only mathematical text. The source supports the caution that symbolic text is not automatically easy for learners.

**Boundary:** It does not measure second-language or interlanguage comprehension.

### Amalric and Dehaene (2016)

**APA-ready reference**

Amalric, M., & Dehaene, S. (2016). Origins of the brain networks for advanced mathematics in expert mathematicians. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America, 113*(18), 4909–4917. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1603205113

**Primary records**

- Publisher DOI: https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1603205113
- Authoritative full-text mirror in PubMed Central: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4983814/

**Claim supported:** In 15 professional mathematicians and 15 academically matched nonmathematicians, high-level mathematical judgments recruited a reproducible bilateral network with essentially no overlap with the sentence-comprehension/general-semantic areas tested. This supports distinguishing expert high-level mathematical reasoning from ordinary-language processing.

**Boundary:** It does not show that language is irrelevant to learning, exposition, definitions, or reading a mathematical book, and it does not estimate interlanguage comprehension.

### Sweller and Cooper (1985)

**APA-ready reference**

Sweller, J., & Cooper, G. A. (1985). The use of worked examples as a substitute for problem solving in learning algebra. *Cognition and Instruction, 2*(1), 59–89. https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci0201_3

**Primary record**

- Publisher DOI: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s1532690xci0201_3

**Claim supported:** Across five experiments, worked examples reduced processing time and subsequent error/solution time for novice algebra learners on structurally similar problems. This supports including worked examples in a self-study product.

**Boundary:** It does not establish that worked examples alone replace a complete curriculum, feedback, or all instruction.

### Pitchford (2015)

**APA-ready reference**

Pitchford, N. J. (2015). Development of early mathematical skills with a tablet intervention: A randomized control trial in Malawi. *Frontiers in Psychology, 6*, Article 485. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00485

**Primary record**

- Publisher full text: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00485/full

**Claim supported:** The single-school randomized trial enrolled 318 Standards 1–3 pupils (283 in the final analysis) and tested an eight-week, Chichewa-language tablet mathematics intervention. Significant gains over standard practice were found in Standards 2 and 3, with Standard 3 transfer to paper-and-pencil assessment. This supports the feasibility of carefully designed direct learner-facing digital mathematics in a lower-resource setting.

**Boundary:** It was an urban-school tablet intervention with class-teacher and technical support. It does not by itself prove that a downloadable book, phone-only delivery, or fully teacher-independent use has the same effect.

### Kleidermacher and Zou (2025)

**APA-ready reference**

Kleidermacher, H. C., & Zou, J. (2025). *Science across languages: Assessing LLM multilingual translation of scientific papers* [Preprint]. arXiv. https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2502.17882

**Primary record**

- arXiv record: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17882 (initial submission 25 February 2025; current v2 updated 4 September 2025)

**Claim supported:** The paper translated scientific articles into 28 languages while retaining JATS structure. Its automated question-answer benchmark averaged 95.9%; the author study found high perceived information retention but also overtranslated terminology, and the paper records terminology inconsistency and truncation risks. It supports the paper's balanced claim that capable LLM translation can preserve much tested content while still requiring terminology and completeness controls.

**Boundary:** This is a preprint, its automated QA is not a proof of source-faithfulness, and it is not specifically a mathematical-document benchmark.

### E Sobhani et al. (2026), MathMist

**APA-ready reference**

E Sobhani, M., Sayeedi, M. F. A., Mohiuddin, T., Islam, M. M., & Shatabda, S. (2026). MathMist: A parallel multilingual benchmark dataset for mathematical problem solving and reasoning. In *Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EACL 2026* (pp. 2524–2550). Association for Computational Linguistics. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.findings-eacl.131

**Primary record**

- ACL Anthology: https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-eacl.131/

**Claim supported:** MathMist contains 2,890 parallel Bangla–English gold-standard artifacts and approximately 30,000 aligned question-answer pairs across 13 languages. Its model evaluations found persistent cross-language inconsistency, with more pronounced degradation in lower-resource settings.

**Boundary:** This is a multilingual mathematical problem-solving/reasoning benchmark, not an evaluation of full-document mathematical translation.

**Name note:** ACL's canonical family-name field is **E Sobhani**, so the current in-text form **(E Sobhani et al., 2026)** is consistent with the proceedings metadata.

### Li et al. (2024)

**APA-ready reference**

Li, Z., Wu, Y., Li, Z., Wei, X., Yang, F., Zhang, X., & Ma, X. (2024). Autoformalize mathematical statements by symbolic equivalence and semantic consistency. In A. Globerson, L. Mackey, D. Belgrave, A. Fan, U. Paquet, J. Tomczak, & C. Zhang (Eds.), *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems* (Vol. 37, pp. 53598–53625). Curran Associates. https://doi.org/10.52202/079017-1697

**Primary records**

- NeurIPS proceedings: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6034a661584af6c28fd97a6f23e56c0a-Abstract-Conference.html
- Microsoft Research description: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/autoformalizing-mathematical-statements-by-symbolic-equivalence-and-semantic-consistency/

**Claim supported:** The paper ranks autoformalization candidates using symbolic equivalence (automated theorem-prover evidence) and semantic consistency (informalization plus similarity), improving accuracy on MATH and miniF2F across the reported models and baselines. This supports adding formal-fragment consistency checks to a broader QA stack.

**Boundary:** It validates mathematical autoformalization candidate selection, not natural-language translation quality by itself.

## Remaining metadata uncertainties

- The individual OECD country notes do not expose individual DOIs; use their stable OECD landing-page URLs. Their listed corporate author is OECD, and all three are dated 5 December 2023.
- The Bangladesh report has no useful bibliographic PDF metadata and no personal-author statement. Corporate authorship by the Directorate of Primary Education is the least speculative choice because the title page and report governance identify DPE/its Monitoring and Evaluation Division as the administering body. The registered government-portal URL refused a direct request during verification on 2026-08-30; the locally preserved report bytes, title page, and cited page were nevertheless inspected directly. A search index also exposed a `www.dpe.gov.bd` alias for the same path, but that hostname failed DNS resolution during the same check, so neither endpoint should be described as currently retrievable without a fresh readback.
- The PBS Table 11 documents do not separately state a publication date; 2023 is the census/data year. If strict APA publication-date semantics are required, replace `(2023)` with `(n.d.)` and add a retrieval date, while retaining “Census-2023” in the title.
- The Kleidermacher and Zou source remains an arXiv preprint; no peer-reviewed venue or external DOI is stated on the current arXiv record.
