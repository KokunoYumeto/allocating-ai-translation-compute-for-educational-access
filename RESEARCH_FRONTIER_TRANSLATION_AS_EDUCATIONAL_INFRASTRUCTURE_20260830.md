# Research-frontier translation as educational and research-capacity infrastructure

Date: 2026-08-30  
Status: evidence memo and companion-paper foundation

## Finding

Postgraduate monographs, seminar literature, and research-frontier corpora are
educational infrastructure. They are also research-capacity infrastructure. They should
not be scored as if they were mass foundational textbooks, but a small direct reader
population does not make an EGA-, SGA-, FGA-, or Stacks-like intervention valueless.

The allocation model therefore needs two parallel lanes:

1. **Foundational access:** large direct learner populations, coherent progression,
   learning and practical capability, prerequisite coverage, and access cost.
2. **Research-frontier capacity:** smaller specialist populations, language burden,
   corpus centrality, prerequisite readiness, reference value, terminological
   infrastructure, downstream generativity, and lifecycle compute cost.

Prestige must not be converted into a fabricated mass reach multiplier. Population reach
must not erase specialist leverage.

## Evidence basis

- UNESCO ISCED 2011 classifies level 8 as advanced study and original research,
  normally culminating in publishable work that contributes significantly to knowledge
  (paras. 259-260). Doctoral research is explicitly education.
- UNESCO's Recommendation on Open Educational Resources defines OER to include
  learning, teaching, and research materials and supports translation, local-language
  adaptation, accessible formats, and offline provision where rights permit.
- UNESCO's Recommendation on Open Science includes multilingual books, reports,
  conference papers, OER, repositories, and long-term infrastructure. Its Recommendation
  on Science and Scientific Researchers treats literature, libraries, and databases as
  part of researchers' continuing learning.
- The UN Committee on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights identifies language as a
  barrier to access to science, calls for open literature access, and warns against
  restricting poorer states to applied rather than advanced science (General Comment
  No. 25, paras. 16-18 and 47-49).
- Amano et al. (2023), surveying 908 environmental researchers, found reported median
  additional English-language reading time of 46.6% for moderate-proficiency and 90.8%
  for low-proficiency nationalities in the specified subgroup. This is discipline-
  specific and self-reported; it is not a mathematics effect size.
- Rostadmo et al. (2021) randomized 129 Norwegian physicians to the same review in
  Norwegian or English. The Norwegian group scored 10.40 versus 9.08, a difference of
  1.32 (95% CI [0.03, 2.62], p=.046). This supports a comprehension mechanism but does
  not establish a frontier-mathematics effect.
- Mueller-Langer et al. (2020) associated OARE access to more than 11,500 journals with
  29.6% more publications, 8.4% more references, and 14.5% more OARE references across
  2,490 institutions. Cuntz et al. (2025) estimated about 41% more institutional
  publications and 21% more clinical trials after HINARI access across 99 eligible
  countries. These are literature-access results, not translation experiments, and show
  complementarity with existing institutional capacity.
- Amano et al. (2021) screened 419,679 papers across 16 languages and found 1,234
  qualifying non-English conservation studies; including them expanded geographic
  coverage by 12-25% and taxonomic coverage by 5-32%. Multilingual research contains
  nonredundant knowledge and translation should be bidirectional.
- The Stacks Project explicitly describes itself as both an open-source textbook and a
  reference for graduate students and researchers, with navigable theorem dependencies.
  Its bibliography connects EGA and SGA to later results, illustrating how a single
  corpus can be instructional and infrastructural.

No direct causal study located in this pass establishes that translating EGA, SGA, FGA,
or a comparable mathematics corpus increases doctoral completion, publications, or
discoveries. The paper must state that limitation.

## Research-frontier measurement dashboard

For each language/corpus pair, report separately:

- addressable master's, doctoral, faculty, researcher, professional, and advanced
  independent-learner populations;
- observed language burden for the actual specialist population;
- corpus centrality in syllabi, dissertations, theorem dependencies, citations, and
  current research tools;
- exact translated-coverage deficit and obsolescence;
- prerequisite readiness from proof through the relevant advanced fields;
- non-overlap with English, another academic lingua franca, a bilingual edition, or an
  evidence-supported interlanguage;
- rights, openness, source visibility, semantic HTML/MathML, tagged PDF, offline
  availability, and stable identifiers;
- formula preservation, theorem/proof alignment, terminology consistency, correction
  density, and reproducible versioning;
- derivative courses, glossaries, annotations, seminar notes, formalizations, search
  tools, and later translations enabled;
- uptake in courses, seminars, theses, publications, citations, and local lead
  authorship; and
- verified translation, correction, maintenance, storage, and delivery cost.

Keep outputs, outcomes, and impacts separate. Pulford et al. (2020) found that only 1%
of outcome/impact indicators in a 668-indicator research-capacity corpus met all four of
their quality criteria, so a component dashboard is safer than one opaque scalar.

## Commissioning rule

Use separate foundational and research-frontier budgets/rankings or publish a Pareto
frontier. A research corpus becomes a strong candidate when it is central, durable,
missing in the target language, legally distributable, connected to an available
prerequisite ladder, source-visible and searchable, and likely to enable substantial
downstream reuse relative to lifecycle compute cost.

Evaluation should compare original-language, translated, and bilingual-aligned versions
among advanced readers, measuring reading time, proposition comprehension, proof
reconstruction, theorem lookup, terminology transfer, and error detection. Later course,
seminar, thesis, and research uptake should inform revisions without acting as a release
hold.
